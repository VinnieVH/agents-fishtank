The creatures

Every Claude Code or Copilot CLI session swims in your tank as one of these. A new session gets the creature and glow colour there are fewest of, so no two look alike, and keeps them for as long as it lives. The helpers it starts (subagents) swim along as small copies. And here's a little about the real animals.

Tropical fish

Not one fish but thousands of kinds, all living on coral reefs. Their bright colours and stripes help them find their own kind and confuse hungry predators, who have trouble picking one fish out of a dazzling crowd.

Koi

Coloured carp, first bred by rice farmers in Japan about two hundred years ago. They're calm, learn to eat from your hand and can live for decades. Hanako, the most famous koi, was said to be over 200 years old.

Sturgeon

One of the oldest fish families alive, around since the time of the dinosaurs. Instead of scales it has rows of bony plates, and it feels its way along the river bed with whiskers under its snout. Sadly, most kinds are now endangered, mostly because of the caviar trade.

Angelfish

Comes from the slow, weedy rivers of the Amazon, where its tall, flat body slips easily between reeds and roots. Pairs often stay together, and both parents guard their eggs and young.

Pufferfish

When threatened it gulps water and blows itself up into a spiky ball too big to swallow. Many puffers are also deadly poisonous. In Japan only specially licensed chefs may serve them, as the delicacy fugu.

Squid

Swims by jet propulsion, squirting water out of a funnel, and escapes behind a cloud of ink. It has three hearts and blue blood. Its giant deep-sea cousins have the biggest eyes in the animal kingdom, as big as a dinner plate.

Shrimp

There are thousands of kinds. Cleaner shrimp run "cleaning stations" where reef fish line up to have parasites picked off. The pistol shrimp snaps its claw so fast that it makes one of the loudest sounds in the sea.

Lobster

Grows by climbing out of its old shell, and keeps growing for as long as it lives, which can be a very long time. A blue lobster is the result of a rare genetic quirk, roughly one in two million.

Anglerfish

Lives in the deep sea, where sunlight never reaches. The glowing lure on its head is lit by bacteria and draws curious prey right up to its huge mouth. In some kinds the tiny male fuses to the female for life.

Lionfish

Those beautiful spines are venomous, so very few predators bother it. Originally from the Indian and Pacific Oceans, it has spread through the Atlantic and Caribbean, where divers are encouraged to catch it. It tastes good, too.

Octopus

Famously clever: it opens jars, solves puzzles and has been known to escape from aquariums. Most of its neurons are in its arms, it has three hearts, and it can change colour and texture in the blink of an eye.

Seahorse

Believe it or not, a fish. Dad carries the eggs in a pouch and gives birth to the babies. Seahorses are poor swimmers that hold on to seagrass with their tails, and some couples greet each other every morning with a little dance.
